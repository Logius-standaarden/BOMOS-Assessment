# BOMOS

## Welcome

**BOMOS: Your guide to successful standards**

BOMOS (Management and Development Model for Open Standards) offers a valuable collection of good practices for setting up standards management effectively. These proven methods apply equally well to individual standards and to complete trust frameworks.

**What BOMOS offers you**

BOMOS gives you not only strategic insight but also concrete tools for developing and managing your standard successfully. The method helps you answer essential questions such as:

- How do you involve stakeholders effectively in your standard?
- How do you ensure that your standard continues to meet user needs?
- How do you organise sustainable financing for your standard?

**The BOMOS assessment**

Our BOMOS assessment gives you a clear picture of the current level of management of your standard or trust framework. For the best result, we recommend completing the assessment together with all parties involved. This not only creates a more complete picture but also offers an excellent opportunity to discuss areas where opinions differ. Completing all modules takes about 30 minutes. The Strategy module is the largest: it takes about 10 minutes.

**How does it work?**

The questionnaire is structured according to the BOMOS Management and Development Model, shown below. This model provides a layered structure of the activities needed to develop and manage an open standard, divided into strategic, tactical and operational layers.

**Need help?**

Stuck, or have questions about certain parts? Please contact the organiser of the assessment or Logius directly. We will gladly help you on your way to a professionally managed standard.

## Explanation of the BOMOS assessment

This assessment consists of statements that you can score for the standard or trust framework in question. The questions are divided into the following parts:

- **Strategy**
- **Tactical**
- **Operational**
- **Implementation support**
- **Communication**

The scores on these parts map the maturity of a standard or trust framework.

## Strategy

Strategy provides direction. Strategic activities are therefore the long-term, direction-setting activities. Think of:

- *Governance*

Governance is the policy on the organisation's own administrative structure and the organisation surrounding decision-making.

- *Vision*

A vision describes the direction in which the trust framework or standard wants to develop. It is the dot on the horizon and serves as the long-term focus.

- *Financing*

Financing is the organisation of a financial model for a trust framework or standard that is viable in the long term.

More information on strategy within BOMOS can be found in BOMOS Part 2: The Elaboration (logius.nl).

**Adoption and Recognition - Insufficiently meets**

**Adoption and recognition**

There are still opportunities for improvement in the area of adoption and recognition.

Working groups of the Forum Standaardisatie have identified a number of critical success factors that played a role in the adoption of various standards:

1. The standard must be mature; otherwise nobody dares to invest.
2. Patience: adopting a standard takes time, sometimes several years.
3. The benefits must be clear to everyone, for the business process, for society and financially; there is support.
4. There must be a committed problem owner, precisely because adoption takes many years; real commitment is indispensable.
5. A critical mass of users is needed.
6. A leading party or a leading process can greatly stimulate adoption.
7. There must be an active community involved in the development and use of the standard.
8. Money is needed for support, training, rewards, and so on.
9. Use a good mix of adoption instruments.

Which element do you see the most opportunities in?

**Tips to get started:**

## Formalise and document the validation and certification process

- Formally lay down the complete validation and certification process, including the criteria, review moments (upon joining, periodically, upon changes) and responsibilities.
- Publish this information in an accessible place, so that (potential) participants know exactly what is expected of them and how the process runs.

## Ensure objective and testable criteria

- Recalibrate the validation and certification criteria: ensure that they are derived directly from the set of agreements and relevant norms (such as information security, interoperability, privacy).
- Make the criteria concrete, measurable and publicly available, so that assessment is not arbitrary and transparency is safeguarded.

## Set up independent assessment

- Appoint an independent party or committee to carry out validation and certification, one that is not involved in the day-to-day management or development of the trust framework.
- Consider outsourcing assessment to external, specialised certification bodies to avoid conflicts of interest and increase credibility.

## Improve and facilitate implementation support

- Develop or update a test or simulation environment where new entrants can independently prepare, test and validate their implementation.
- Offer extensive support such as sample files, clear documentation, workshops and a helpdesk, so that participants are well prepared for the validation and certification process.

**Complaint Handling - Insufficiently meets**

**Complaint handling**

It is good to have a reporting point for complaints. It is also important that it is clear who can report complaints and at which point of contact. There are also differences here between the management of a standard and that of a trust framework. Complaints about a standard will usually end up with the management organisation itself, because it manages the specifications.

With a trust framework this can of course also happen, but there is a greater likelihood of complaints arising from services that the trust framework offers. It is then logical for such complaints to go to the party with which the user has concluded a contract. Complaints may also arise among the participants, and here too it is good to agree in advance how to deal with them.
