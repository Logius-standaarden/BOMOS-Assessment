BOMOS

Welcome

BOMOS stands for Beheer en OntwikkelModel voor Open Standaarden. In English this means Management and Development Model for Open Standards.

BOMOS is a collection of good practices for effectively managing a Standard. These good practices can also be applied to the management of a Trust Framework.

BOMOS provides strategic insights as well as practical tips for creating a successful Standard or a Trust Framework. It offers guidance on questions such as:

  • How do you engage stakeholders?
  • How do you ensure that your Standard or Trust Framework consistently meets user needs?
  • How do you arrange funding?

This BOMOS assessment provides insight into the level at which the management of a particular Standard or Trust Framework is organised. It is best to complete this assessment with parties involved in the Standard or Trustframework and discuss those aspects where differing opinions exist.

The questionnaire's structure is based on the activities model depicted in the image below. If you encounter difficulties or do not understand a question? Please contact the organiser of the assessment or Logius (Logius | Contact met Logius).

For more information; see BOMOS: The Foundation 3.0.1

Strategy

Strategy provides direction. Strategic activities are those activities that set the direction for the long term. These include:

  • Governance

Governance refers to the way an organization manages its own administrative structure and decision-making processes.

  • Vision

The financial model of a Standard  or Trust Framework that is viable in the long term.

3. The funding model is future-proof and can grow along with the number of participants.

A good funding model for a set of agreements should have the following characteristics:

Proportionality: The financial contribution of participants is proportional to their size, use, or benefit derived from the scheme

Flexibility: The model can adapt to both growth and decline in the number of participants without jeopardising the stability of the system

Balanced distribution: There is a balance between fixed and variable costs, so that the system does not become overly dependent on a small number of large participants.

Predictability: Participants have insight into their future financial obligations, which helps them with their own planning.

  • Patience: Adoption of a standard takes time, sometimes several years.
